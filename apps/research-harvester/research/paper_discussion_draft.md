# §5  Discussion

## 5.1  Summary of findings

This study set out to ask four questions of the secondhand market: whether it can be made
to yield a brand-level durability measure at all (RQ1), whether what it yields agrees with
what laboratories find (RQ2), which of the two selection forces — the flight of lemons or
the parade of survivors — governs its composition (RQ3), and whether the answers travel
across a border (RQ4). The market, like most witnesses, answered some questions plainly,
answered others in ways that required interpretation, and to one question gave an answer we
did not expect but should have. Four findings organise the testimony.

**First, the proxy is constructible and internally coherent (RQ1).** From 17,483 pooled
listings we derived three signals — functional-survival ratio (S1), hedonic residual-value
premium (S2), and old-vintage share (S3) — for 20 brands with n ≥ 100, with classifier
validation (brand F1 = 0.98, condition F1 = 0.77–0.94) meeting conventional thresholds, and
near-perfect ranking stability across three data revisions (ρ ≥ +0.98). The composite ranking passes face
validity: the BSH group and Liebherr occupy the upper half; Indesit and Whirlpool anchor the
bottom, mirroring long-standing consumer-test evidence.

**Second, external validity is signal-specific, not uniform (RQ2).** H1 — a positive,
significant rank correlation with independent reliability data — is supported for S3
(ρ = +0.68 against Warentest overall grades and +0.78 against its handling sub-grade, both
CIs excluding zero, n = 13) and for S2 (ρ = +0.67 against dTest overall scores, CI
excluding zero, n = 21; a correlation that strengthened monotonically across data
revisions). It is *not* supported for S1, which tracks none of the four test programmes
(its wave-1 borderline correlation with dTest fell monotonically as the sample tripled).
The validation pattern is itself informative: the market's stock composition (S3) carries
the cleanest durability information; the market's *prices* (S2) partially incorporate tested
quality alongside a distinct brand-equity component; and the functional mix (S1) is so
shaped by retention and repair economics that it measures the condition of the circulating
stock rather than underlying reliability. A single-signal proxy would have obscured this
decomposition — the multi-signal design is what makes it observable.

**Third, both selection mechanisms operate simultaneously, segmented by brand (RQ3).** The
hedonic model's positive old-age coefficient (+0.21 log-points, stable across categories
and every data revision) is a market-wide survivor-selection premium: buyers pay *more* for machines that have
already demonstrated longevity, the opposite of standard depreciation. Yet the null
correlation between S1 and lab-tested endurance (ρ = −0.10, n = 12) — registered ex ante as
a mechanism probe rather than a convergent-validity test — reveals why survivor selection
cannot be read directly off the working share: brands with identical lab-perfect endurance
scores (Miele, Liebherr, LG, Samsung, Privileg, Haier all grade 1.0) span nearly the full
observed S1 range. Machines that
fail late but are kept until death (Miele) and machines that fail early and are dumped broken
(Bauknecht) both depress or compress S1, through opposite channels. The appliance resale
market is thus neither a pure lemons market nor a pure survivors' market; it is a mixture whose
composition varies by brand-specific retention and repair economics.

**Fourth, cross-border consistency remains unresolved (RQ4) — and we now know why.**
ρ(CZ, DE) = +0.15 with a wide CI (n = 18). Crucially, more than doubling the Czech sample
via a repaired source adapter did not move the CZ S1 ceiling: Czech sellers rarely list
appliances described as broken on any platform, so the Czech working share is uninformative
at any sample size. The obstacle is structural (listing culture), not statistical; H2 must
be tested through price-based signals or defect-carrying Czech channels (e.g., repair-shop
auctions), not through S1.

## 5.2  A two-dimensional quality space

A machine can fail its owner in two quite different ways: it can stop working, or it can
stop being worth fixing. The most consequential null results in this study — BDP ⊥ ORDS
repairability (all |ρ| < 0.22) and BDP ⊥ the official French repairability index (all
|ρ| ≤ 0.19) — say that these two failures are, across brands, statistically unacquainted:
"product quality" in the circular-economy sense is not a scalar. Two unrelated
repairability sources, one crowd-sourced and one regulatory, sit orthogonal to the BDP. Our
data locate every brand in a two-dimensional space:

- **Durability** (does it keep working?) — measured by S3 (and, with mechanism caveats, S1),
  validated against lab tests;
- **Repairability** (is it worth fixing when it stops?) — measured by ORDS fix rates and
  median repair age, uncorrelated with the first dimension.

The four corners are all populated: Bosch and Siemens are durable *and* repairable; Hisense
and Samsung survive but resist economical repair; Gorenje breaks but gets fixed (60% ORDS fix
rate); Indesit and Whirlpool break early and are discarded. This matters for policy because
the EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation treats durability and repairability as
separate ecodesign parameters — our results confirm empirically that they must be measured
separately, and demonstrate that a single secondhand-market cross-section can measure the
first dimension while open repair data measures the second. Neither substitutes for the other.

The price signal adds a third, economic layer with a two-part message. Resale prices do
incorporate quality information — S2 correlates significantly with dTest scores (ρ = +0.67) —
so the market is not blind to durability. But the incorporation is partial and distorted by
brand equity: the largest premium in the data (Hisense, +0.57 log-points) attaches to a
mid-table test performer, while the lab leaders Liebherr and Miele carry no premium at all.
If secondhand prices were used naively as a quality signal — as some circular-economy
monitoring proposals suggest — they would rank the average brand roughly correctly and the
most heavily marketed brands badly wrong. The BDP framework's contribution here is
diagnostic: it separates the quality-tracking component of prices from the brand-equity
residual.

## 5.3  Reinterpreting the proxy: effective market durability

The Miele and AEG discordances, corroborated by three independent sources (Warentest owner
survey, Warentest lab endurance, ORDS repair ages), force a precise statement of what BDP
measures. It is not absolute engineering reliability. It is **effective market durability**:
the durability of the brand's stock as it actually circulates in the secondhand economy, after
retention behaviour and repair economics have filtered which units appear at all.

We argue this is a feature for the intended application, and the point deserves an
illustration. Consider three machines. The first serves one household faithfully for
seventeen years and goes to scrap without ever meeting a second owner. The second serves
two households for nine years each, changing hands in the middle. The third fails in year
six and is landfilled. A laboratory endurance rig cannot tell these three lives apart — on
the test bench, the first machine is simply the best. But for material flows, for the
circular economy's central ambition of keeping goods in use (Stahel, 2016), the three
trajectories are entirely different objects, and it is the *market* record — who lists
what, at what age, in what condition, at what price — that distinguishes them. The proxy
should be read as a market-flow indicator complementary to, not competing with,
type-approval-style lab testing; it measures the durability the economy actually receives,
which the theory of §2.1 reminds us is an equilibrium outcome, not an engineering constant
(Waldman, 2003).

## 5.4  Methodological contribution and transferability

The pipeline costs are a fraction of laboratory or survey alternatives: one harvest window,
open-source scrapers, and ~10,000 enriched listings produced brand rankings that correlate
significantly with test programmes whose per-product lab costs run to five figures. Three
design choices drove this and transfer to other product categories:

1. **Within-brand ratios (S1) self-normalise for market share**, removing the need for sales
   denominators that are commercially confidential.
2. **Regulatory discontinuities as vintage instruments** — the March 2021 EU energy-label
   rescaling gives a sharp, category-specific age band requiring no seller-stated age. Any
   category with labelling regime changes (tyres, light bulbs, boilers) admits the same trick.
3. **Multi-signal triangulation with pre-registered mechanism nulls** — treating predicted
   non-correlations (S1 × endurance, BDP × ORDS) as evidence about mechanisms rather than
   validation failures.

## 5.5  Limitations

**Survivor bias is the object of study, not a removable nuisance — but it limits claims.** We
cannot recover absolute failure-time distributions from a cross-section; brands whose broken
units never reach the market (discarded, exported, cannibalised) have upward-biased S1 (AEG),
and brands with long retention have downward-biased S1 (Miele). All rankings are of the
*listed* stock.

**Short panel.** Two waves nine days apart establish ranking stability and yield a first
turnover statistic (45% of listings persist across nine days), but the horizon is far too
short for cohort survival curves or sell-through modelling; waves spaced months apart would
convert S3 into a genuine cohort-depletion measure.

**Brand-level correlations are fragile at n ≈ 20.** The wave-1 S1 × dTest correlation
(+0.43) fell to +0.21 on doubled data (§4.8). Only replicated findings — the S3 lab-quality
correlations, S2 × dTest, the age premium, and the composite ranking — should carry
inferential weight; we flag this openly as a discipline for this class of designs.

**Czech condition-statement culture** caps the cross-border analysis (§4.7): even with 28%
of the analysis pool now Czech, CZ S1 is ceiling-bound because broken units are rarely
listed there. Pooled S1 results are therefore effectively DE-identified.

**Endurance validation is confined to washing machines** — Warentest publishes endurance
sub-ratings for no other appliance category in our extract — and to n = 12 brands, several
represented by a single tested model. The endurance-related conclusions are correspondingly
tentative.

**Brand ≠ product line.** Brand-level aggregation collapses heterogeneous product tiers
(Bosch Serie 2 vs Serie 8) and OEM arrangements (BSH producing for multiple badges; Whirlpool
group badging Bauknecht, Privileg, Indesit). Some of the "brand equity" in S2 may be
tier-composition effects.

**Classifier attenuation.** Broken-listing recall of 0.63 attenuates S1 levels uniformly (broken precision is 1.00 — no false broken labels);
rank ordering is preserved under the (untestable) assumption that recall does not vary by
brand. Listings in dialect or with sparse descriptions are the main failure mode.

## 5.6  Policy implications

For ESPR implementation and the Digital Product Passport, three implications follow — and
they are practical rather than utopian, which is perhaps the best that can be said of any
policy implication. (i) A market-based durability indicator of this kind can be produced
continuously and cheaply, making it suitable for *monitoring*: not a replacement for the
laboratory, but a tireless assistant that reads the classifieds every month and tells the
laboratory where to look (cf. Polverini, 2021, on the ecodesign framework's chronic
shortage of verifiable durability metrics). (ii) The double-penalty cluster — low
durability, low repairability, low lab scores — is identifiable from public data alone; a
screening rule at the observed gap in our composite (BDP_z < −2) isolates exactly the
brands independent testing also ranks last. (iii) Because S2 mixes tested quality with
brand equity, price-based instruments (e.g., VAT differentiation on secondhand goods) would
advantage durable brands only imperfectly and would over-reward the heavily marketed;
stock-composition instruments target durability more cleanly.

## 5.7  Future work

Priorities, in order: accumulation of the now-automated monthly waves into a genuine panel
(the nine-day two-wave comparison already demonstrates feasibility and 45% listing
persistence; wave-3 hypotheses are pre-registered, including a listing-survival test);
a Czech defect-carrying channel (e.g., repair-shop and bazar-výkup auctions) to break the
CZ S1 ceiling; acquisition of brand-level unit-sales shares to re-express S3 as a true
survival rate in the registration-data tradition (the analysis script and data template
ship with the replication package); extension of capacity parsing to non-washing-machine categories to improve
category-level hedonics; acquisition of dTest's annual reliability-survey index (0–100,
subscription) for a second inferential survey-based validation at n ≥ 15 brands; and a
model-level (rather than brand-level) replication in one category, which would separate
tier-composition effects from genuine brand effects in S2.
