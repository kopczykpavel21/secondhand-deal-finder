# Literature Gap

## Direct answer

The paper-level gap is:

> There is no credible Czech-market study that combines second-hand vehicle
> listings with historical cohort volumes to estimate brand/model durability
> signals while adjusting for age, mileage, and market size.

## Step-by-step reasoning

### 1. What the existing used-vehicle literature mainly does

Most of the used-vehicle literature focuses on:

- adverse selection
- lemons problems
- information asymmetry
- seller signaling
- residual values and sorting

That literature is relevant, but it is not the same as measuring a Czech
brand-level durability proxy.

### 2. What the appliance/electronics literature mainly does

The appliance/electronics literature is much weaker for this exact question.
The uploaded syntheses point in a consistent direction:

- repairability
- circular economy
- reuse behavior
- consumer perceptions

Those topics matter, but they do not solve the identification problem for a
brand durability measure because the market often lacks:

- manufacture year
- standardized model identifiers
- denominator data by brand/model/cohort

### 3. What is missing in the Czech context

A brand can appear frequently in second-hand listings for two very different
reasons:

- it was originally sold in large numbers
- it survives better into older cohorts

A durability paper has to separate these two.

That is exactly where the contribution lies:

- normalize used-market presence by original cohort size
- condition on age and mileage
- combine survival-style presence with price and defect signals

### 4. Why this is defensible as a Q1 contribution

The contribution is narrow enough to be credible:

- one country
- one product domain
- one carefully framed proxy

It is not claiming to solve durability in general. It is claiming to build a
market-based, empirically tractable **durability signal** in a domain where the
required variables are actually observable.

## Working positioning sentence

Use some version of this in the introduction:

> Existing research on used vehicles provides strong evidence on adverse
> selection and information frictions, but it does not offer a Czech
> brand-level durability proxy that normalizes second-hand market presence by
> cohort size, age, and mileage.
